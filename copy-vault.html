send a photo, get a colour-by-number kit for the markers you already own. 👋

built for alcohol markers (ohuhu, copic) and coloured pencils (prismacolor, polychromos). nothing to mix, no water, no palette, no brushes to wash, no waiting for anything to dry. print it and uncap a marker.

you pick the photo, your dog, your wedding, the trip you still talk about, the house you grew up in. I turn it into a custom colour-by-number kit you print at home (or at any print shop) and colour in yourself. no drawing skill needed: if you can match a number to a number, you can do this.

and because every colour comes with its exact hex and RGB, you can match it to whatever is already in your pen cup, whatever the brand.

WHAT YOU GET (5 printable PDFs, 46 pages for the 19-colour kiwi kit in the photos)

• the outline, 5 pages, not 1. four line weights, faint through medium, numbered or plain. this matters more with markers than with paint: alcohol markers are see-through, so a heavy line or a printed number will still be sitting there when you're done. print the faint, unnumbered one to colour on, keep the numbered one next to you as your map, and your finished piece has no numbers showing anywhere.

• every region carries a number, including the slivers too small to hold one, which get a thin pointer line drawn in from outside. nothing is left for you to guess at.

• the canvas grid page, the fifth outline, over a red inch-by-inch grid, for copying onto stretched canvas or a sketchbook page by hand. you are not stuck at printer size.

• the swatch card, and this is the one that matters if you own markers. every colour prints as a true 1-inch square with its name, its exact hex and RGB, and its coverage in square inches. print it on the same paper you'll print your outline on, then swatch your own markers right beside each square and write the codes in. now you have a matching key made on your paper, with your printer, for your collection. no guessing off a screen, where nothing matches anyway.

• the colour-by-colour guide, one page per colour, that colour alone on the page. wondering where every #12 hides? turn to page 12. with markers this is the page you actually work from: you find every region of one colour and lay them all down while the nib is still wet, which is exactly when alcohol markers blend cleanly into each other.

• the build-up guide, the same one page per colour, but each page shows the whole painting as it will look once that colour is down. you watch it arrive before you start.

• the finished preview in full colour, plus a second copy with the numbers printed right on the colours, so you can check yourself halfway through without unrolling anything.

WHY THIS ONE, NOT A FREE GENERATOR

• free sites give you one outline with no colour names at all. you get four outlines, the grid page, a measured swatch card, and two more pages of step-by-step guidance for every single colour in your painting.

• free sites give you no hex values, so you cannot match a single colour to a single marker without eyeballing it off a screen. every colour here comes with its hex and RGB.

• free sites drop the numbers on regions too small to hold them. this one draws a pointer line to every one.

• the colours run lightest to darkest, which is the order markers and pencils actually want. you can always go darker over a light colour, you can never go lighter. the numbering does that thinking for you.

• I check your photo will work before I build anything, and swap it free if it won't. no site does that.

GOOD TO KNOW BEFORE YOU PRINT (markers)

• print the swatch card first, on the exact paper you plan to use, and scribble a marker on it. that one test tells you two things at once: which of your markers match, and whether your printer's ink smears. some inkjet inks lift when alcohol hits them; laser and toner prints don't move. if yours smears, a print shop or a laser printer solves it for a dollar or two.

• marker paper or smooth cardstock beats copy paper. alcohol goes straight through thin sheets, so put a scrap behind it either way.

• coloured pencils are happiest on toothier cardstock, and they want the medium outline, not the faint one.

HOW IT WORKS

1. order, then send me your photo in a message

2. I check it’s a photo that will paint well, if not, I’ll ask for another (no charge)

3. your kit arrives in your inbox within 3 to 5 days

4. print it, uncap your markers, and go one number at a time

GOOD TO KNOW

• sizes from 4x6 inches up to 16x20 inches, tell me where it’s going and I’ll recommend one

• between 10 and 40 colours depending on your photo. fewer colours if you're working from a small marker set, more if you own hundreds. tell me roughly what you have and I'll build to it.

• your swatch card tells you the piece count before you start (the kiwi kit in the photos is 19 colours and 410 pieces), and it flags in writing any piece smaller than a size-0 brush tip, which is about a fineliner's worth. those are the spots you'll want your smallest nib or a sharp pencil for. I'd rather you know than find out on a sunday afternoon.

• built for alcohol markers and coloured pencils, and it works just as well with acrylics or watercolour if that's what you reach for

• this is a digital kit, nothing ships, nothing to wait for at the door

photos that paint best: good light, one clear subject, not too far away. when in doubt, send it over, I’ll tell you honestly.

on paint words: the shop is called hello paint and always will be, and "paint by number" stays in titles and tags because it's what people search. but in the body of anything, don't assume a brush. say colour it in, fill it in, work through it. "the painting" is still fine for the finished thing, plenty of marker people call it that. "grab your paints", "mix", "brush", "let it dry" are not fine: our customer has none of those and picked us so they wouldn't need them.

brands we can name: ohuhu and copic for alcohol markers, prismacolor and polychromos for pencils. name them plainly, we're not affiliated with any of them and we don't pretend to be.

spelling: “colour” everywhere, always. lowercase everywhere, always (even at the start of sentences), except the pronoun “I”, which stays capitalized (autocorrect fights it otherwise, and it reads as a typo lowercase). the only emoji is the wave 👋.

the three rulespin this above the desk

1. lowercase always, the name is “hello paint”, never “Hello Paint”, never “hellopaint” (except in handles). the one exception across all copy: the pronoun “I” stays capitalized.

2. no orange, anywhere, not in graphics, not in photo grading. it’s the one colour the brand never wears.

3. show the before & after, the photo next to the painting is the most convincing thing we own. lead with it.
